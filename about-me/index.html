01 · Who I Am

Most of what I know came from doing the work.

After graduation, I spent about three years working with data: Excel cleaning, merging, batch adjustments and SQL report development. During those years I saw many kinds of messy data and many different reporting requirements.

From that point on, I was no longer dealing only with data. I started dealing with the business processes and systems behind it.

02 · Data → ERP

Data makes more sense when you understand the business behind it.

Once I started working on ERP projects, I began to understand more clearly how businesses actually operate.

Why can the same type of product master data require completely different classifications in two companies?

Why do finance, purchasing, sales, inventory and invoicing need to connect in a particular way?

Who should maintain a field? How detailed should a piece of master data be?

Quite often, the software itself is not the hardest part. The harder part is understanding how the company actually works, then turning that into data and processes that a system can understand.

First understand the real problem. Then decide what tool should be used.

04 · After ERP

From large systems back to smaller problems.

After ending the stage of my career focused mainly on ERP delivery, I spent two years applying what I had learned to smaller and lighter problems.

I gradually realised that some small businesses simply do not need one, let alone several, large business systems.

What they often need is something affordable, practical and simple enough to solve the problem in front of them.

That is why many things I built during these two years were not created because I wanted to “make a product”. They started because someone had a specific pain point.

OneSheet CRM

Some clients are businesses of only two or three people, including the owner. They still need CRM functions, but may not need or be ready for a commercial CRM platform. I designed OneSheet CRM so they could manage customers, follow-ups and relationships directly in Excel at very low cost.

Google Apps Script Cold Email Automation

One client was an individual freelancer who still needed a repeatable way to reach potential customers. I built an automated workflow using Google Apps Script, Notion and email. It reads customer records from Notion, fills predefined email templates, sends messages, updates customer status and sends a result report after completion.

EWSI

A small photography team needed to process large numbers of images repeatedly, so I built EWSI to batch-convert image formats and dimensions instead of handling the files one by one.

Excel Merged-Cell Tool

A client regularly ran into problems caused by merged cells in Excel, so I created a small tool to split those merged areas and rebuild the data into something easier to process.

Multi-Country Browser Clock

I also built a browser-based multi-country clock for a small cross-border business, making it easier to see the local time of customers in different markets.

Small businesses do not always need a bigger system. Sometimes they just need someone to solve the specific problem in front of them.

05 · Small-Business Technology

Small-business technology needs have changed.

In the past, companies often needed people to maintain mail servers, file servers, internal applications and other IT infrastructure.

Today, Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, SaaS products, cloud services and managed platforms handle much of that infrastructure.

The technology problems faced by many small businesses have therefore changed as well.

Excel and CSV files that need to be cleaned and reorganised every week.
Data that still has to be copied manually between Notion, email, Google Sheets and other applications.
AI tools that have already been purchased, but are not yet connected to the actual workflow.
A specific problem where existing software is unsuitable, but building an entire system would be excessive.

Traditional IT focused heavily on keeping systems running. Small businesses today increasingly need their existing tools to do more of the work for them.

08 · Business Systems

How I think about implementing business systems.

Because I spent years delivering ERP systems, I also want to say something about how I see companies implementing business systems.

When a company starts needing ERP, CRM or another business system, the first thing I usually look at is not the software itself.

It is the data.

I have always thought that implementing an enterprise system is a little like building a house.

The software may be the structure above ground, but customers, vendors, products, BOMs and other master data are the foundation.

Every company manages its business differently. Even the same type of data may require completely different levels of detail, classifications and field structures.

That is why master data often goes through repeated cleaning, confirmation and adjustment before a system can go live.

In ERP projects, this work can consume a great deal of time for both the customer and the implementation team.

If a company is preparing to implement a new system, I would rather help prepare this data properly in advance, so implementation consultants can spend their time on processes, configuration and decisions that actually require their experience, instead of repeatedly reorganising Excel files.
